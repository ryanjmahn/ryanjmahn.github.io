OpenWM

leading technical development · with wenhao · sep 2026 → now

world models trained on narrow hardware niches, so an engineer can check a design's real-world performance in milliseconds instead of waiting on a full simulation. fast is only useful if people can trust it, so we study where these models break.

our first paper, surrogate hacking in LLM-driven airfoil design (in preparation), looks at what happens when an AI designer optimizes against a model's mistakes instead of the physics. openwm grew out of probix labs, which pivoted in sep 2026, and is backed by a $2,500 grant.

Contaminated Ancestors: seven ways our own evaluation protocol nearly told us the wrong thing

2nd author · neurips 2026 tae workshop · ai4science workshop (accepted) · may → sep 2026

trust in machine learning for science starts with the evaluation. we built a method for catching a silent failure in ancestral protein reconstruction, then audited the evaluation protocol we had used to test it. the audit found ways a protocol can be locally correct and still globally misleading: weak baselines, too-thin controls, unchecked conventions and silent hardware errors. the paper ends with an 11-question checklist any lab can run against its own protocol.

The specialist tool lost on its own home turf: benchmarking pocket predictors on KRAS G12D

sole author · mentor: prof. se-jin hwang, hanyang univ. college of medicine · under review, columbia junior science journal · jun → sep 2026

KRAS G12D, the mutation behind most pancreatic cancers, has a cryptic pocket that only forms as the protein flexes. i benchmarked three MD-free tools at finding it (fpocket, P2Rank and AE-PocketMiner) on two crystal structures and a 24-step ladder of conformers generated from the closed one alone.

the cryptic-pocket specialist came last: pure geometry localized the pocket best (0.95 MCC vs 0.12). by detection rate alone, both readings would have been backwards. a tool's label isn't evidence; you have to check it on the case that matters.
