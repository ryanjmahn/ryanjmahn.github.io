Contaminated Ancestors: seven ways our own evaluation protocol nearly told us the wrong thing

2nd author · neurips 2026 tae workshop · ai4science workshop (accepted) · may → aug 2026

we built a method for catching a silent failure in ancestral protein reconstruction, then audited the evaluation protocol we had used to test it. the audit found ways a protocol can be locally correct and still globally misleading: weak baselines, too-thin controls, unchecked conventions and silent hardware errors. the paper ends with an 11-question checklist any lab can run against its own protocol.

The specialist tool lost on its own home turf: benchmarking pocket predictors on KRAS G12D

sole author · mentor: prof. se-jin hwang, hanyang univ. college of medicine · under review, columbia junior science journal · jul → sep 2026

KRAS G12D, the mutation behind most pancreatic cancers, has a cryptic pocket that only forms as the protein flexes. i benchmarked three MD-free tools at finding it (fpocket, P2Rank and AE-PocketMiner) on two crystal structures and a 24-step ladder of conformers generated from the closed one alone.

the cryptic-pocket specialist came last: pure geometry localized the pocket best (0.95 MCC vs 0.12). by detection rate alone, both readings would have been backwards.
